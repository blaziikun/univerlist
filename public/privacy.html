Privacy and terms

Last changed 4 November 2026. Plain words, and true to what the code does: it's all open source, so you can check.

Without an account

Everything you add stays in your own browser. Nothing is sent to this site. When you look something up (a cover, a film's details), your browser asks that catalogue directly (AniList, Open Library and the others), or, for the few that need a secret key (TMDB for films and TV, IGDB for games), asks through this site's /api, which adds the site's key and passes the question on. It keeps no record of who asked; the answer itself (a film's details, never anything about you) is kept for a day so the same lookup isn't made twice. If you add your own key or proxy, your browser uses that instead. A few sites refuse to share their pictures with web pages; for those, the picture is fetched through wsrv.nl, a free image proxy, which then sees that picture's address and your IP address. You can switch that off (Settings › Data, sources & keys › Image proxy). The built-in themes' fonts come with the app; only if you pick a different font for a theme of your own is it fetched from Google Fonts, which then sees your IP address. Those catalogues, wsrv.nl and Google have their own privacy policies.

Cloudflare, who host it

The site runs on Cloudflare. Like any web host, Cloudflare sees your IP address to deliver the site and protect it (for example, to slow down an address that sends too many requests), and keeps basic request logs for a few days. See Cloudflare's privacy policy.

Your choices

  • See and take everything: Settings › Data, sources & keys › Download everything gives you your whole library as one file.
  • Delete everything: Settings › Account & sync › More › Delete my account removes your account and every copy in the cloud at once. Your library on your devices stays.
  • Questions: open an issue on GitHub.

Under UK and EU data protection law, the account data is kept to provide the service you asked for, and the daily counts and sign-in list to keep it secure. You can also complain to the ICO.

Accessibility

UniverList aims to meet WCAG 2.2 AA. Everything can be used with a keyboard, with a visible focus outline; dialogs return focus to where you were; there's a reduced-motion setting, and your system's reduce-motion setting always wins; text scales with your browser's zoom. Every page and the main dialogs are checked automatically for accessibility problems on every change.

Known gaps: reordering by drag has keyboard alternatives (Move up and Move down) but not on every list yet, some pictures from the catalogues have only their title as a description, and a few small, secondary details (counts and dates beside a title) are lighter than the 4.5:1 contrast the rest of the text meets. If something gets in your way, open an issue on GitHub and it will be fixed or explained.
